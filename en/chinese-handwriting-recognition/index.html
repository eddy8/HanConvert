Chinese handwriting input online

Find a Chinese character by drawing it online

Use this Chinese handwriting recognition tool when you know what a Hanzi looks like but not its Pinyin or how to type it. Draw the Chinese character with a mouse, trackpad, touchscreen or pen, then choose from the closest matches.

Live character candidates

The list refreshes after each completed stroke and shows up to ten likely Hanzi. Undo the last stroke whenever the shape goes wrong.

Useful character details

Choose a match to see its English meaning, common Pinyin readings, radical, structure, stroke count and Unicode, then copy it or open the animated stroke-order lookup.

Recognition runs in your browser

Stroke coordinates are not uploaded to JianFan.app. A public WebAssembly component performs the matching on your device.

How to use Chinese handwriting input

  1. Draw one Chinese character in the centre of the grid, one stroke at a time.
  2. Choose the closest Hanzi from the automatically updated matches.
  3. Check its meaning and Pinyin, or continue to the animated stroke-order page.
  4. To type several characters, choose Add to text after each one; the Pinyin of the phrase appears below the text box, and you can copy it all at once.

Chinese handwriting recognition questions

How do I find the Pinyin of a character I cannot read?

Draw the character and select the closest candidate. The result shows its English meaning, Pinyin, radical, structure, total strokes and Unicode, with a link to stroke order.

Can I see what a Chinese character means?

Yes. Select a candidate and the result shows a short English meaning from the Unicode Unihan database, together with Pinyin, radical, structure and stroke count. For example, 好 shows “good, excellent, fine; well”.

Can I use it as a handwriting input method?

Yes. After each character, select the candidate and choose Add to text; the pad clears for the next character. When you finish, choose Copy next to the text box to copy the whole phrase. The Pinyin of the whole phrase, read word by word, appears below the text box, for example 银行 reads yín háng. The page recognizes one character per drawing, not joined-up sentences.

What if the character I want is not among the candidates?

Redraw it larger with clearly separated strokes. If it still does not appear, search by its components in Chinese character lookup. For printed text in a photo or screenshot, “Chinese character recognition from photo” is faster.

Does it work on phones and tablets?

Yes. Draw with your finger or a stylus directly in the square. No app installation or sign-up is needed.

How can I find a Chinese character if I do not know its Pinyin?

Draw the character shape in the writing grid and select the closest result. You can then check its Pinyin, stroke count, Unicode and stroke order without knowing its pronunciation first.

Can I draw a Chinese character with a mouse or finger?

Yes. The writing grid works with a mouse, trackpad, touchscreen or digital pen on desktop, tablet and mobile browsers.

Does stroke order affect recognition?

The matcher uses stroke direction, position and count. Small stroke-order differences may still work, but a large, clear character written in the usual order normally produces better matches.

Does it recognize Simplified and Traditional Chinese?

It covers more than 9,000 common Simplified and Traditional characters. A few rare variants or Japanese-only forms may be unavailable.

Can it recognize a photo or a handwritten sentence?

No. This tool recognizes one character drawn on the writing pad. It is not photo OCR or continuous handwriting recognition.

Is my handwriting uploaded?

No. Matching runs in the browser. Only the public recognition files and stroke-order data for a selected character are requested remotely.
