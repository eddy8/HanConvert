LOOK UP AN UNKNOWN KANJI BY DRAWING

Draw a Kanji when you can see it but cannot read it

To read a Kanji you do not know, draw it here and pick the matching candidate: the On and Kun readings appear immediately. It works for print books, signs, screenshots, and any text you cannot copy, and continues to radical, stroke count, and Japanese stroke order.

How to find the reading of a Kanji by handwriting

  1. Draw the Kanji in the square, one character at a time, with a finger, stylus, or mouse.
  2. Candidates appear automatically as you write; pick the matching character from up to 10 candidates.
  3. Read its On-yomi, Kun-yomi, radical, total strokes, and English meanings.
  4. For a word of two or more Kanji, select Add to text for each character; the reading of the whole word appears in Hiragana below the text box.

About 6,400 Japanese Kanji

Reference shapes are generated from KanjiVG (CC BY-SA 3.0) Japanese stroke data, so besides the Joyo characters the tool recognizes hard-to-read Kanji such as 嘘, 苺, 鰻 and 牡蠣, and Japanese-made characters such as 込, 畑 and 峠.

Candidates while you write

Recognition runs in a background thread after every stroke, so there is no button to press, and the exact stroke order or count is not required.

Handwriting stays in your browser

Recognition data loads from this site, and your strokes are matched in the browser instead of being sent to a recognition API.

Kanji handwriting recognition questions

How do I find the reading of a Kanji I cannot read?

Draw the character and select the matching candidate to see its On and Kun readings. Open the Kanji dictionary from the result to check name readings (nanori), radical, and stroke count.

Can I look up the reading of a word with two or more Kanji?

Yes. Select Add to text after each recognized character to build the word; the reading of the whole word appears in Hiragana below the text box, for example 躊躇 reads ちゅうちょ. For longer passages, paste the text into Kanji to Hiragana & Furigana.

What if the Kanji I want is not among the candidates?

Recognition covers the about 6,400 Kanji in KanjiVG, including the Joyo characters and most name and non-Joyo Kanji. If redrawing larger with separated strokes does not help, search the Japanese Kanji dictionary (over 13,000 characters) by radical and stroke count, or find the character by its parts in Chinese character lookup.

Does it work on phones and tablets?

Yes. Draw with your finger or a stylus directly in the square. No app installation or sign-up is needed.

Must I use the correct stroke order?

Not exactly, although closer stroke direction, shape, and placement generally produce better candidates.

Why does the first recognition take longer?

When the page opens, about 1 MB (compressed) of Japanese reference shapes loads in the background; anything you draw before it finishes is recognized as soon as it is ready, and browser caching helps later visits. Word readings need a one-time download of a Japanese dictionary of about 16 MB.

Is my handwriting uploaded?

No. Strokes and matching stay in this browser, and word readings are analyzed in the browser too. Only the public recognition data and dictionary files are downloaded.
